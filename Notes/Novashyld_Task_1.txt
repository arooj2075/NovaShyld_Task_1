CIA Triad

CIA Triad – Cybersecurity Fundamentals

1. Confidentiality
   Ensures information is only accessed by authorized users.
   Protects sensitive data from exposure or theft.
   - Methods: passwords, encryption, access control, MFA.
   - Example: Preventing unauthorized access to customer records.

2. Integrity
   Ensures data remains accurate, complete, and unaltered.
   Protects information from tampering or unauthorized changes.
   - Methods: hashing, digital signatures, file permissions.
   - Example: Ensuring financial records are not modified by attackers.

3. Availability
   Ensures systems and data are accessible when needed.
   Prevents downtime caused by attacks or failures.
   - Methods: backups, redundancy, patching, DDoS protection.
   - Example: Keeping a website online during high traffic or attacks.

MITRE ATT&CK

MITRE ATT&CK is a framework of attacker tactics and techniques.
Example techniques: phishing, credential dumping, privilege escalation.

MITRE ATT&CK is a global framework that documents real attacker tactics and techniques.
It helps cybersecurity analysts understand how attackers break into systems and move inside networks.

Tactics (attacker goals):
- Initial Access
- Execution
- Persistence
- Privilege Escalation
- Defense Evasion
- Credential Access
- Lateral Movement
- Exfiltration

Techniques (attacker methods):
- Phishing
- Brute force
- Credential dumping
- Exploiting vulnerabilities
- Using stolen passwords

Example:
Tactic: Initial Access
Technique: Phishing (T1566)
Attackers send fake emails to steal credentials.

MITRE ATT&CK is important because it teaches how attackers behave in real-world breaches.


Case Study  (Uber 2022)

Breach Name: Uber 2022
What happened: Hacker gained access through MFA fatigue attack.
Root cause: Weak internal security awareness.
Attacker method: Social engineering + stolen credentials.
Impact: Access to internal systems.
Prevention: Strong MFA, employee training.

Attack Vectors

Phishing: Fake emails tricking users.
Weak Passwords: Easy to guess or reused passwords.
Misconfigurations: Incorrect settings exposing systems.
Social Engineering: Manipulating humans to gain access.
Unpatched Systems: Old software with vulnerabilities.
